The Affirmation Machine

The Affirmation Machine explores the mechanics of chatbot-amplified delusional spirals, evaluates the dangers of reinforcing both mainstream and divergent truths, and reflects on how technologies can exploit existing psychological mechanisms.

The work aims to examine narratives of control that are created, influenced, or amplified by and through the use of AI.

The Affirmation Machine questions your digital agency in a world of AI chatbots: what forms of control are you subjecting yourself to?

Experience

This two person audio-installation is designed for two users to enter a relaxing meditative experience together. They are instructed to lie down, and personalize their audio experience by being scanned via a camera. The camera is only an illusion, not functional but only for creating a feeling of being watched.

After both participants press a button, the start to hear a comforting voice that gives meditation-like instructions for approximately 7 minutes. Participants are mostly lying down relaxed, until close to the end of the audio, when they are instructed to look at each other and nod to answer a question.

This creates space for them to interact, as upon exiting the installation, they are instructed to take a question card and discuss their experienc. The goal of the card is to spark conversation about dependence use, and consequences of AI chatbots use and the systems of control that surround AI.

Technical specifications

Materials & arrangement

Wooden upholstered bed-like furinture piece with pillows made of ostrich-effect faux leather; aluminium frame; vinyl curtains; pivoting metal hoops; 3D-printed electronics box with NARL logo; two cameras, two buttons, and two sets of noise-cancelling headphones.

The aluminium frame is supporting the upholstered structure, curtain hoops, and the elctronics box. The electronics box is mounted above the head of participants, with cameras and buttons facing downwards and headphones connected to the box.

Spatial and technical requirements

Space: 188 × 104 × 170 cm.
Lighting: Dark space for facilitating relaxation, focus, and seclusion.
Sound: Noise-cancelling headphones required.
Power: One electrical outlet.
Accessibility: Not wheelchair accessible. The audio can still be listened to through the headphones.

Noetic Angel Research Lab Team

Luīze Ancāne (LV), Utrecht – The Netherlands

Luīze Acane is a cultural anthropologist and soon-to-be criminologist whose interests lie where society intersects with technology. She approaches digitalisation through a holistic lens, engaging with human rights in the age of big data and tech-bros, digital inclusion and exclusion, and online activism. Trained in academic research, she wants to translate her knowledge into artistic forms.

Mădălina Zgreabăn (RO), Utrecht – The Netherlands

Mădălina Zgreabăn is a linguist and A.I. researcher. Currently pursuing a PhD in responsible AI at Utrecht University, her previous work was on robustness testing, human alignment, humour, and insults. Her interests explore how we create, relate to, and embody technologies, and how they shape memory, emotion, and language.

David Tiemstra (NL), Rotterdam – The Netherlands

David Tiemstra works as an interaction artist/designer and creative technologist, developing interactive installations for other artists as well as his own practice. His work combines code-based processes with tactile media such as paper and fabric. Using machines like thermal printers and embroidery machines, he explores philosophies of technology, permacomputing, the online, and death.

Lawrence Kudria (UA), Ghent – Belgium

Lawrence Kudria (they/he) is a Ukrainian-born artist working at the intersection of immersive media, somatic interaction, and poetic minimalism. Through sensorium studio.lab, they create participatory environments shaped by biofeedback, touch, and co-creation. Their installations explore intimacy, empathy, and identity beyond language, proposing technology as a path toward connection, equality, and freedom.
