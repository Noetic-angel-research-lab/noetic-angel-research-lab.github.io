Policy

The Affirmation Machine is relevant for current discussion on how AI should be regulated in multiple regards. 

What is manipulation? Does a system that excessively approves the user’s opinions also count as manipulation?

The project addresses how the definition of ‘manipulation’ in the EU AI policy act is not nuanced enough. AI systems are built to please users, thereby providing a perfect context for personally induced delusional spirals which do not correspond to the traditional notion of external manipulation, or what the EU act calls: “purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques”, or “causing them to take a decision that they would not have otherwise taken”. 

Labeling AI content is also insufficient. Our project clearly shows, by means of the AI personalised scan conducted in the beginning, that the comfort and environment of the technology are designed to surpass its danger and encourage relationships and, consequently, anthropomorphism. To avoid cases of AI psychosis or other types of AI systems of control, a change in AI models is needed, if not a full ban. When user awareness alone is not enough, change should come from the providers and regulators of AI content. 

What do we focus on?

Current AI policies heavily focus on the strategic protection of AI knowledge and infrastructure as part of a broader digital autonomy procurement. For example, the new Dutch coalition agreement of the cabinet Jetten speaks about digital safety and digital autonomy. Strategic deployment of AI is strengthening competitiveness, enhancing AI sovereignty, and ensuring the safe and responsible use of AI are the three key points in this strategy. The starting point for this agreement is the procurement of European digital infrastructure, guided by principles of security-by-design, zero-trust, sovereignty, open source, and supply chain security. When speaking about risks, geopolitical tensions are the main source of concern. The mitigation of these risks is. Digital autonomy is more than just AI sovereignty and European digital infrastructure. Digital autonomy also includes the psychological effects of AI use and infrastructure. 

The use of AI, and specifically AI chatbots, can cause adverse effects on the individual due to cognitive offloading and, in turn, create larger societal hardships. The dependency we as a society have been developing to use AI chatbots for our daily questions, but more importantly, the dependency we have been developing to view AI chatbots as our friends, romantic partners, and spiritual guides, is concerning. So when we speak about digital agency, Europe-wide, but also in the Dutch context, we must include the social aspects of AI.
